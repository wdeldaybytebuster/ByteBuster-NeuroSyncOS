# 7. Background Watcher

## What this screen is

Background Watcher is the part of the app that keeps an eye on things in the background. It is meant to notice, suggest, and prepare, not to jump in and take over.

In technical terms, this screen is also called ScoutDaemon. From the user’s point of view, it is the lookout.

A simple way to think about it is this: Background Watcher is the person on the roof watching the horizon. It can see things worth noticing, but it does not do the ground work by itself.

This screen is useful when you want to:

- see what the app has noticed,
- review suggestions or alerts,
- check background activity,
- understand whether something worth attention is happening.

## What you can see on it

### Dashboard page

The dashboard page focuses on what the watcher has observed.

You may see:

- observed activity,
- notices or suggestions,
- system awareness information,
- status updates about background behavior.

The exact details depend on what the watcher has picked up. Some of the time, nothing unusual may be happening, and that is normal.

#### Observed activity

This shows things the watcher has noticed. It may include status changes, unusual events, or other signals that something deserves attention.

Not every notice means something is wrong. Some notices are just awareness.

#### Suggestions

Background Watcher may suggest next steps or flag things that might need a human look. These are suggestions, not orders.

That is an important difference. A suggestion means “this might be worth checking.” It does not mean “this must be done now.”

#### System awareness

The watcher can help the app notice conditions around it. That can include things like system state or activity that may matter later.

This helps the app make better choices about what to do next, but it does not mean the watcher is deciding everything.

### Set-up page

The Set-up page for this screen is about how the watcher behaves and what kinds of awareness it provides.

You may see settings that affect:

- how the watcher watches,
- what kinds of events it pays attention to,
- how it presents notices or suggestions.

The exact controls depend on the screen, but the general idea is the same: you are shaping how much the watcher notices and how it tells you about it.

## What you can do on it

On Background Watcher, you can:

- see what the watcher has noticed,
- read notices or suggestions,
- check background activity,
- review system awareness information,
- adjust watcher settings if the screen provides them.

## How it works

Background Watcher works by observing and reporting. It does not usually act as the main worker.

Its job is to:

- notice things,
- pass along signals,
- suggest that something may be worth checking,
- help the rest of the system stay aware.

That separation matters. If something needs to happen, the watcher may point at it, but the actual action usually belongs to another part of the app.

This is intentional. It keeps the system calm and predictable. One part notices. Another part acts.

## How to use it in real life

Use Background Watcher when:

- you want to see whether anything unusual has happened,
- you want a second set of eyes on the system,
- you want to check suggestions before acting on them,
- you want to know whether the app has noticed something important.

A good habit is to treat notices as early hints. They are useful, but they are not the whole story.

Another good habit is to compare a suggestion with the screen where the work would actually happen. That helps you decide whether the suggestion is worth following.

## Small tips

- Do not assume every notice is urgent.
- A suggestion is not the same thing as a command.
- If the watcher is quiet, that can be normal when nothing unusual is happening.
- If something seems important, check the screen that would actually do the work.
- Use the watcher as awareness, not as the final decision-maker.

# 6. Approvals and Tools

## What this screen is

Approvals and Tools is the part of the app that helps you decide what should happen next. It is built around one big idea:

**nothing important runs unless a person says yes.**

In technical terms, this screen is also called PortGrid. From the user’s point of view, it is the checkpoint, the permission board, and the tools hub.

This screen is useful when:

- a plan is waiting for your approval,
- something needs permission before it can proceed,
- you want to see what the app is trying to do,
- you want to open a project terminal,
- you want to manage connections to other devices,
- you want to see recent tool activity or workspace items.

## What you can see on it

### Dashboard page

The dashboard page is built around a few main areas.

#### Workflow map

This shows the steps in a planned job. Sometimes it shows a draft plan that is waiting for approval. Sometimes it shows an older run so you can look at what happened.

If a proposal is pending, the map may show the steps in a visual form and also as a simple list. That makes it easier to read what the job would do.

#### Needs your approval

This is the most important safety area. It lists things that need a person to look at them before they go ahead. Low-confidence or risky items usually show up here.

You may see both small approval items and larger workflow proposals here. Each one may include:

- what needs approval,
- why it needs approval,
- how confident the system is,
- a way to approve or decline.

The point of this area is not to annoy you. The point is to make sure the app does not rush into something important without a person checking it.

#### Trust and safety badges

These are little signs that tell you how much the system trusts a plan. They are not magic. They are signals.

They help you decide whether to slow down and look more closely. If a badge says something is high confidence, that is still a signal, not a promise.

#### Recent tool activity

This shows a short list of things that have recently been done or checked. It helps you see what has been happening.

#### Embedded terminal

This is a small command-line window for the project you have chosen. It is meant for you to open, not for the app to open on its own.

When you close it, the app may scan the project for changes. That is useful because agents or other tools may have made edits while the terminal was open.

#### Sync peers

This is about connecting with other devices. The rule here is simple: a device is not connected until you approve it.

Discovered devices wait first. Approved devices are added to your trusted list. You can also add a device manually if you want to.

#### OKF workspace widget

This area shows workspace-related items or activity for the project. It helps you see what is happening in the current workspace context.

#### OKF mindmap

This is a view that can help you see relationships or structure in a more visual way. It is useful when you want a bigger-picture view instead of just a list.

### Set-up page

The Set-up page for this screen is mainly about permissions, tools, and control settings.

#### Approval controls

This is where the screen’s approval system lives. It includes things like:

- the list of items waiting for attention,
- the ability to approve or decline items,
- the ability to approve or decline proposals,
- the ability to handle several items together when appropriate.

The important idea is that approval is a choice, not a formality. When you approve something, you are letting it proceed.

#### Tool and workspace controls

This area may include controls for workspace display, mindmap viewing, and related workspace features. These help you shape how the screen shows information.

#### Terminal controls

This is where you open or close the embedded terminal for the project. The terminal is a tool for you, not something the app starts on its own.

#### Sync peer controls

This is where you manage devices you want to connect with. You can:

- approve discovered devices,
- reject discovered devices,
- add a device manually,
- view your approved list.

The key rule is consent. A device should not connect until you approve it.

## What you can do on it

On Approvals and Tools, you can:

- look at a workflow proposal before it runs,
- approve a proposal,
- reject a proposal and send it back,
- approve or decline smaller approval items one at a time,
- approve several safe-looking items together when the screen allows it,
- look at recent tool activity,
- open a project terminal,
- close the terminal,
- approve, reject, or manually add sync peers,
- view workspace items,
- open a mindmap view,
- review trust and safety badges.

## How it works

Approvals and Tools works by combining three ideas:

1. visibility,
2. consent,
3. control.

Visibility means you can see what the app wants to do. Consent means the app asks before doing something important. Control means you can say yes, no, or not yet.

When a plan is waiting, the screen shows it as a proposal. You can read the steps and decide whether to approve it. If you approve it, the work can move forward. If you reject it, it goes back for more work or review.

Approval items may come from different places, but they share the same basic idea: someone needs to check them before they go ahead. Some items may be low confidence and need more attention. Others may be high confidence and easier to approve.

The terminal is separate from approvals. It is a tool you open when you want a command-line session for the project. Closing it may trigger a scan so the app can pick up changes.

Sync peers work the same way as approvals in one important sense: a connection should not happen until you approve it. Discovered devices wait in a pending list until you decide what to do with them.

## How to use it in real life

Use Approvals and Tools when:

- a new plan is waiting,
- something needs your permission,
- you want to see what the app is trying to do next,
- you want to open a terminal for a project,
- you want to connect or disconnect from another device,
- you want to review recent activity.

A good habit is to read the workflow steps before you approve. Another good habit is to slow down if a plan is long, complicated, or risky.

If you are not sure, it is okay to reject and look again later. Approval is not a test you have to pass quickly. It is a choice.

## Small tips

- Do not rush approvals just because the app looks confident.
- If a proposal looks risky, read the steps carefully.
- If you are unsure, reject and revisit the plan.
- Treat the terminal as your tool, not the app’s automatic workspace.
- Remember that sync peers should be approved before they connect.
- Use the trust badges as hints, not as final answers.

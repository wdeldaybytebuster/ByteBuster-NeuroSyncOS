# NeuroSync Sovereign OS — User Manual

## 2. PortGrid — the control and approval hub

PortGrid is the part of the app that helps you decide what should happen next. It is built around one big idea:

**Nothing important runs unless a person says yes.**

A simple way to think about it is this: PortGrid is the front desk, the checkpoint, and the permission board all in one.

### Main parts of PortGrid

- **Workflow Map**
  This shows the steps in a planned job. Sometimes it shows a draft plan that is waiting for your approval. Sometimes it shows an older run so you can look at what happened.

- **Needs Your Approval**
  This is the most important safety area. It lists things that need a person to look at them before they go ahead. Low-confidence or risky items usually show up here.

- **Trust and Safety Badges**
  These are little signs that tell you how much the system trusts a plan. They are not magic. They are signals. They help you decide whether to slow down and look more closely.

- **Recent Tool Activity**
  This shows a short list of things that have recently been done or checked. It helps you see what has been happening.

- **Embedded Terminal**
  This is a small command-line window for the project you have chosen. It is meant for you to open, not for the app to open on its own. When you close it, the app may scan the project for changes.

- **Sync Peers**
  This is about connecting with other devices. The rule here is simple: a device is not connected until you approve it. Discovered devices wait first. Approved devices are added to your trusted list.

### What you can do in PortGrid

- Look at a workflow proposal before it runs
- Approve a proposal
- Reject a proposal and send it back
- Approve or reject smaller approval items one at a time
- Approve several safe-looking items together
- Look at the recent tool activity
- Open a project terminal
- Approve, reject, or manually add sync peers

### A friendly warning about approvals

If a proposal looks risky, PortGrid may ask you to slow down. In some cases, it may ask for extra confirmation before the job can run. That is normal. It is there to make sure a big action is intentional.

Also, approval is not the same as “the app guesses it is fine.” Approval means you chose to let it proceed.

### Good habits here

- Read the workflow steps before you approve.
- If a plan is long or complicated, take a minute.
- If you are not sure, reject and look again later.
- Do not rush approvals just because the app looks confident.

### When to use PortGrid

Use PortGrid when a new plan is waiting, when something needs your permission, or when you want to see what the app is trying to do next.
